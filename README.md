<h2>Project Overview</h2>

This project analyzes global happiness data from the 2015 and 2022 World Happiness Reports to examine changes in happiness across countries and regions.

The analysis evaluates overall happiness alongside six contributing factors: GDP per capita, family/social support, life expectancy, freedom, generosity, and trust/government corruption. The datasets included 158 ranked countries in 2015 and 146 countries in 2022.

Using Google Sheets, Excel, and Tableau, I cleaned and standardized the datasets, conducted comparative analysis, and developed visualizations to identify geographic and socioeconomic patterns in global happiness.

<h2>Project Objective</h2>

The analysis sought to answer:

- How did global happiness scores change between 2015 and 2022?
- Which countries and regions reported the highest happiness scores?
- How did the six contributing factors vary across regions?
- What geographic and socioeconomic patterns emerged from the comparison?

<h2>🧹 DATA PREPARATION: World Happiness Datasets</h2>

<h3>Analysis Approach</h3>

The 2015 and 2022 datasets were structured differently, requiring standardization before comparison. Headers in the 2022 dataset were reformatted for consistency with the 2015 dataset, and a Region field was added to support regional analysis.

<h3>Analytical Value</h3>

- Standardized data across reporting periods
- Enabled comparison between 2015 and 2022
- Supported regional aggregation
- Prepared the datasets for visualization and analysis

<h2>📊 EXPLORATORY ANALYSIS: Regional Happiness</h2>

<h3>Research Question</h3>

How did happiness and its contributing factors vary across global regions?

<h3>Analysis</h3>

Google Sheets was used to create pivot tables and visualizations comparing regional performance across the six happiness factors.

The analysis identified Australia and New Zealand, North America, and Western Europe among the regions with higher scores across several factors associated with overall happiness.

<h2>🌎 GEOGRAPHIC ANALYSIS: Happiness by Country</h2>

<h3>Research Question</h3>

What geographic patterns emerge when happiness scores are compared across countries?

<h3>Analysis</h3>

Tableau geographic visualizations were developed to compare Happiness Score by Country in 2015 and 2022, providing a visual representation of the distribution of happiness across the two reporting periods.

<h3>Analytical Value</h3>

- Identified geographic patterns in happiness
- Compared country performance visually
- Highlighted differences across regions
- Made international data easier to interpret

<h2>📈 COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS: 2015 vs. 2022</h2>

<h3>Research Question</h3>

How did happiness and its contributing factors change between 2015 and 2022?

<h3>Analysis</h3>

The analysis found an overall decline in happiness scores while individual contributing factors did not move uniformly. Some economic and social measures increased, while trust in government, freedom, and generosity declined.

This comparison demonstrated the importance of examining multiple contributing factors rather than interpreting happiness through a single measure.

<h2>🔎 Key Findings</h2>

- Australia and New Zealand, North America, and Western Europe demonstrated comparatively high happiness performance.
- GDP/economic conditions, social support, life expectancy, and freedom contributed to perceptions of overall happiness.
- Overall happiness scores declined between the two reporting periods.
- The six underlying factors did not change uniformly between 2015 and 2022.
- Improvement in individual factors did not necessarily correspond with improvement in overall happiness.
